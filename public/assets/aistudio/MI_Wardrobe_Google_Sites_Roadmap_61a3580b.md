# MI Wardrobe Google Sites Prototype Roadmap

Create an English, single-page product showcase that invites visitors to experience MI Wardrobe at an expo and submit a sales enquiry. The wardrobe is the visual centerpiece. Its digital collection, outfit assistant and automated garment retrieval explain why it matters.

This is a build plan, with suggested copy and acceptance criteria. No website has been published. Estimated effort is five working days after core assets and product decisions are available.

## 1. Product story and source decisions

Suggested positioning: **A wardrobe that helps you decide what to wear.**

Suggested supporting copy: “Explore a new way to organise your clothes, plan your look and bring your chosen pieces within reach.”

Use MI Wardrobe consistently as the public name. The English manual uses SmartWardrobe, so confirm the final brand before preparing artwork.

The English DOCX explicitly describes a university project concept. Its proposed mechanisms and screen copy are design evidence, rather than proof of a finished commercial product. Present the page as a prototype showcase and label concept renders and simulated demonstrations beside the relevant media.

| Topic | Evidence and decision for the prototype |
| --- | --- |
| Mirror door | Requested product direction. The supplied sketch shows a small touchscreen instead. Confirm the intended mirror/display arrangement and produce a matching concept render before making it the hero. |
| Digital collection and retrieval | Described in the English manual. Show the intended browse, select and collection flow; identify simulated steps. |
| AI outfit assistance | Central to the user brief. Demonstrate one specific supported interaction rather than implying a complete autonomous stylist. |
| Outfit preview | Described in the English manual using a personal photo. Treat visualisation as a concept until demonstrated; avoid promising accurate fit or sizing. |
| Setup | Use the English manual as the primary detailed journey: account, garment registration, photo upload and confirmation. Validate which steps are implemented. |
| Integration differences | The multilingual Markdown describes MI Home, 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi, humidity sensors and calibration. The English manual describes a separate app/website and Quick Share. Resolve these differences before publishing integration claims. |
| Capacity | Mini: approximately 40 upper and 20 lower positions. Pro: 50–60 upper and approximately 30 lower positions. These are indicative figures from supplied concept materials. |
| Dimensions | Sketch: 180 cm wide × 220 cm high × 95 cm deep. The sketch does not establish separate dimensions for Mini and Pro; do not assign these measurements to both models. |
| Price and availability | Unconfirmed. Use “Pricing to be announced” and “Contact sales” until approved prices, currency and availability are supplied. |

Do not infer voice control, weather integration, accurate body measurement, climate treatment, delivery timing or certifications from the concept.

Keep the visitor journey on one landing page. Interpret the requested header as the hero area, with no separate navigation menu. Add a dedicated visit section before the contact form.

| 2 | Key features — “Your collection. Your daily look. Within reach.” | Three image-and-text blocks: browse your digital collection, explore outfit suggestions, retrieve selected garments. Keep descriptions to one or two sentences each. | Understand the product's everyday value. |

Show only interactions supported by the recording or clearly label a storyboard. Add a brief first-time setup explanation below. | Understand daily use and onboarding. |

Suggested operating copy: “Browse your collection on the built-in display. Explore a look using your saved garments. Select your pieces and collect them at the wardrobe opening.” Use future or concept wording for steps not implemented.

First-time setup should be a short expandable block rather than the main sales story: connect and create an account; register clothes one by one; add a personal photo for the proposed preview feature; review and finish. Confirm whether the photo is mandatory before describing it that way publicly.

## 4. Visual direction

Use warm off-white backgrounds, dark charcoal text and one restrained accent colour. Pair the technology with natural room materials so the product feels appropriate for a home. Use a clean sans-serif font available in Google Sites, large short headings and generous spacing.

Make the wardrobe the largest object in the hero. Keep its silhouette, mirror and garment opening legible. Put the headline and buttons in native text blocks so they remain readable and accessible on phones. Avoid placing essential copy inside the image.

Alternate lifestyle scenes with hardware and interface details. Do not use generic AI artwork as the primary product image. Maintain the same wardrobe design across hero, cutaway and room scenes.

Use a still hero image for the first prototype and a user-controlled demo video below. Treat animated backgrounds, scroll-driven 3D and a functioning AI mirror demo as later experiments requiring separate feasibility work.

## 6. Contact form and visit details

Recommended fields:

- Name and email: required.
- Interest: home use, business use, expo demonstration or other.
- Model: Mini, Pro or not sure.
- Message: optional.
- Company and phone: optional, if useful for sales follow-up.

Use a plain submit label such as “Send enquiry.” Suggested confirmation: “Thank you. We have received your enquiry and will contact you using the details provided.” State a response time only after the team commits to it.

Put a short explanation of enquiry-data use and a link to the applicable privacy information beside the form. Keep any optional marketing signup separate. The lead form should not request personal body photos or measurements.

Store responses in a Sheet with access limited to the people handling enquiries. Set up and test the owner's response notifications. Avoid settings that require visitors to belong to your organisation; verify access while signed out.

The expo venue, address, dates, opening hours and booth number are currently missing. Use clearly marked placeholders in the working draft. Replace them before public launch. If no visit venue is confirmed, describe the visit as an invitation to register interest rather than displaying an invented address or map pin.

## 7. Google Sites implementation

1. Create a blank site and one landing page. Establish the custom theme, brand colour and typography first.
2. Remove the default page header if needed and construct the hero as the first content section. Keep policy pages out of navigation; verify the resulting menu on desktop and mobile. Google documents removing headers and hiding secondary pages, but the home page itself cannot be hidden from navigation. See [page and header controls](https://support.google.com/sites/answer/98216?hl=en).
3. Build the sections with native text, images, buttons and column layouts. Keep one clear heading per section and a sensible reading order when columns stack.
4. Add heading destinations for Contact sales and Visit us. Once the page has a test publication, copy the actual published heading links into the buttons. Use Google-generated links rather than inventing fragment names. Test that each button lands on its intended heading and check whether it opens another tab. See [published heading links](https://support.google.com/sites/answer/6372880?hl=en).
5. Insert the Google Form and Google Map using native controls. Upload the demonstration clip to an approved host such as YouTube or Drive and embed it with viewer permissions that work for the intended audience. Local computer file paths cannot serve as public video links. Google supports [Forms, maps, videos and embeds](https://support.google.com/sites/answer/90569?hl=en).
6. Use a poster image and a concise text explanation around the video. Check its mobile height and player controls. Provide captions or an adjacent transcript where needed.
7. Keep the first release within native Sites layouts. Treat any custom interactive mirror as an isolated embed requiring separate development and testing; do not assume embedded code can restyle or control the whole Google Sites page.
8. Preview desktop, tablet and phone layouts. Validate the published site while signed out because editor preview does not establish public asset access. See [previewing and publishing](https://support.google.com/sites/answer/6372880?hl=en).

The roadmap does not require a fully functional AI backend, commerce checkout or interactive pricing configurator. The prototype's job is to explain the product and collect interest.

## 8. Footer scope

For the prototype, prepare original, applicable pages or links for privacy information, terms for using the site and company/project contact details. Add product support and hardware documentation when available.

Do not copy Square's footer wholesale. “Square Capital, LLC Licenses” belongs to Square. Government licences, consumer health privacy, Trust Center and hardware compliance links should appear only when MI Wardrobe has relevant information to publish. Do not imply certifications that have not been obtained.

Define the actual use of analytics, videos, Maps and Forms before choosing cookie/privacy wording. A “Cookie preferences” link must open a working preference mechanism; a decorative link does not provide that function. Review the live Google Sites and third-party embed behaviour before public launch.

The estimate assumes one builder and timely decisions. Asset production and product uncertainty can extend it; they are the main dependencies.

## 11. Acceptance criteria and evaluation

- Within ten seconds, a new visitor can explain that this is a smart wardrobe with a proposed mirror assistant and automated storage.
- The wardrobe is visually dominant on desktop and phone; no essential hardware detail is lost to cropping.
- Both hero buttons lead to the correct section. No placeholder address, price or dead legal link remains in the public version.
- The video is playable while signed out. Simulated interactions and concept renders are labelled at the point of viewing.
- The Mini/Pro comparison is readable as native text and distinguishes upper from lower capacity.
- A test enquiry submits successfully, appears in the response Sheet and triggers the intended notification.
- The map shows the confirmed location and directions can be opened. Event times and access instructions are explicit.
- Text contrast, image descriptions, headings, keyboard access and form readability have been checked.
- Five representative visitors attempt three tasks: explain the product, compare the models and find how to visit or enquire. Record confusion and revise the page before release.

For the prototype, track valid enquiries and demo interest as the main outcomes. Review form completions and ask visitors what brought them to the showcase. Add analytics only after deciding what is necessary and confirming the platform's privacy and measurement behaviour; do not promise detailed click or video events without a tested implementation.
